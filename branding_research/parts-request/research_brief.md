## 2. Buyer Persona

### Roles & Decision-Makers

| Role | Title | Pain Points | Buying Triggers |
|------|-------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Owner** | Larry (Owner/Operator) | No web presence, losing sales to NAPA/O'Reilly online ordering, can't compete with national chain websites, wants to modernize but doesn't know how | Slow season, competitor opens nearby, customer asks "do you have a website?" |
| **Counter Manager** | Parts Counter Lead | Spends 20+ min/day on phone taking parts requests, handwriting orders, no online catalog, customers frustrated by busy signals | End-of-month inventory, weekend rushes, regular commercial accounts wanting to text/email orders |
| **Commercial Account** | Local garage/mechanic owner | Calls in parts orders between jobs, wants to text part numbers, needs fast turnaround confirmation, tired of being put on hold | Regular ordering cycle, emergency repairs (daily), bulk weekly orders |
| **DIY Customer** | Weekend mechanic | Drives 20 min to store only to find part not in stock, wants to check availability online first | Car repair project, seasonal maintenance (tune-ups, brakes before winter) |

### Demographics
- **Geography:** Ashtabula County (primarily Ashtabula city, Saybrook, Conneaut, Geneva)
- **Business density:** ~40-50 auto repair shops in county, ~8,000-10,000 DIY car owners
- **Income:** Working class, industrial/manufacturing background
- **Vehicle mix:** Domestic trucks/SUVs (Ford, Chevy, Dodge) ~60%, Import (Honda, Toyota, Nissan) ~25%, Domestic cars ~15%

### Buyer Psychographics
- **Value-driven** — price-sensitive, wants to match or beat NAPA/O'Reilly prices
- **Loyalty-minded** — prefers buying local, knows the counter staff by name
- **Frustrated by tech** — many independent owners are older, intimidated by "building a website"
- **Word-of-mouth dependent** — reputation is everything, no marketing beyond signs and referrals

## 3. Auto Parts Niche Definition

### Primary Niche: Domestic & Light Truck Parts (Retail + Commercial)
- **Focus:** Domestic vehicle parts (Ford, Chevrolet, Dodge/RAM, GMC)
- **Commercial accounts:** Local garages, repair shops, fleet maintenance
- **DIY retail:** Walk-in counter sales, phone-in parts requests
- **Key categories:** Brakes, suspension, engine components, electrical, filters, batteries, oils/fluids

### Secondary Niche: Agricultural & Marine Parts (Regional Fit)
- Ashtabula County is #1 in Ohio for grape production, has active Lake Erie fishing/boating community
- Tractor, farm implement, and marine engine parts are a differentiator from national chains
- **Parts request system should accommodate** part numbers, year/make/model queries, and VIN-based lookups

### Not Targeting:
- High-performance / racing parts (too niche for Ashtabula market)
- European luxury imports (low density in area)
- Collision/body shop parts (separate supply chain)

- **RockAuto** — online-only, deep catalog, lower prices

- **Same-day availability** without shipping wait
- **Personal relationships** with every mechanic in town
- **Delivery** to local garages within Ashtabula
- **Auto Value network** provides competitive pricing close to national chains

## 5. Creative Direction

### Brand Positioning Statement
> "The auto parts counter that knows your car, your town, and your name. Ashtabula's local source for the parts that keep this county moving."

### Visual Direction

| Element | Direction | Rationale |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| **Color Palette** | Racing Red (#CC0000), Charcoal Gray (#333333), Metallic Silver (#C0C0C0), White (#FFFFFF) | Auto parts industry standard — red conveys speed/urgency/power, gray/silver communicate industrial reliability. Contrasts well with national chains' blue/yellow schemes. |
| **Typography** | Bold industrial sans-serif — Barlow Condensed (headings), Rajdhani (body) or IBM Plex Sans (body fallback) | Condensed sans-serif matches auto parts catalog aesthetic, fits dashboard/order screens, bold weights convey confidence |
| **Logo** | Gear/wrench icon + wordmark "Lakeshore Auto Parts" | Gear motif signals mechanical, wrench icon is universally understood for auto parts. Could also work as "PARTS REQUEST" for the ordering system brand |
| **Hero Image** | 1280x720 — Auto parts counter at local shop, shelves of parts, warm lighting, mechanic handing part across counter | Must feel real and local — not a sterile showroom. The authenticity gap vs. national chains is the selling point |
| **Mood** | Gritty, industrial, blue-collar, confident | Ashtabula is a working-class Lake Erie town. The brand should reflect that, not try to look like Silicon Valley |
| **Texture** | Slight grain, warm undertones | Analog warmth to differentiate from slick national chain sites |

### Copy Tone & Voice

| Attribute | Guideline |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Tone** | Direct, confident, no fluff. Blue-collar straightforward. |
| **Voice** | "The counter guy who actually knows his stuff." |
| **Key phrases** | "Fast turnaround", "Quality guaranteed", "Ask for Larry", "Ashtabula's own", "The parts you need, when you need them" |
| **Trust signals** | "Serving Ashtabula County since [year]", "Auto Value quality parts", "Locally owned & operated", "We deliver to your shop" |
| **CTA language** | "Request Parts", "Check Availability", "Get a Quote", "Commercial Accounts Welcome" |

### Buyer Pain Points to Address in Copy
1. "Tired of being put on hold?" → Online parts request form
2. "Need a part after hours?" → 24/7 parts request portal
3. "Don't know the part number?" → Describe what you need, we'll find it
4. "Commercial account? Skip the line" → Dedicated commercial ordering
